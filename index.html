HydroSense — smart drip irrigation for terraced farms

Every drop, right where the roots need it.

On sloped and terraced land, water runs off before it can soak in. HydroSense reads soil moisture in three separate zones, checks the weather forecast, and waters the driest zone first in short pulses — so the water goes into the soil instead of down the hill. The ESP32 makes every watering decision itself, so the farm keeps working when the internet drops; the cloud only gives advice.

Team LeaderApp DeveloperSystem Designer
  • Led the four-member student team and designed the system architecture the rest of the build follows.
  • Wrote the ESP32 firmware (Arduino C++) — a 60-second control loop, driest-zone-first scheduling, and pulse and volume caps that keep water below the soil’s soak-in rate.
  • Built the bilingual app in English and বাংলা with Capacitor, synced through Firebase and shared as an Android APK.

Sense

Capacitive probes in each of the three zones are read through a 16-bit ADS1115 ADC, because the ESP32’s own ADC2 can’t be used while Wi‑Fi is on. A DHT22 tracks temperature and humidity, and hall-effect flow sensors confirm how much water was actually delivered.

Decide

Every 60 seconds the ESP32 checks each zone against its dry threshold and looks at the live Open-Meteo forecast. If rain is on the way, it skips watering. If not, the driest zone goes first.

Water

A 12 V diaphragm pump — switched by a MOSFET with a relay as a hardware enable — feeds pressure-compensating drip emitters. Short pulses with volume caps stop the water from running off the slope.

Sync & learn

Readings sync to Firebase over a 4G router, and a 5-day FAO-56 water plan tunes itself to the field in about two weeks. The plan only advises: the ESP32 always applies the smaller of its own limit and the plan’s target.

Predictive water-demand engine

Plans irrigation five days ahead and learns the field it is installed in — without ever taking control away from the controller.

JavaScript · pure engine, no DOM or Firebase dependencies

JavaScriptFAO-56Open-MeteoERA5
  • Layer 1: a 5-day FAO-56 water balance for each zone that produces an advisory watering plan.
  • Layer 2: learns three bounded factors from soil-probe history — crop coefficient Kc, VPD sensitivity and the ET₀ blend weight — starting from FAO-56 book values and settling in about 14 clean days.
  • Layer 3: a day-of-year demand curve built from 10 years of ERA5 climate data.
  • Safe by design: the ESP32 applies min(local cap, plan target), discards plans older than 36 hours, and the plan can never loosen local limits.
  • Ships with a standalone demo page that fetches the live Open-Meteo forecast and exports CSV.
